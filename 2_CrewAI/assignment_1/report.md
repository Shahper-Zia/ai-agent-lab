# Logistics Analysis Report

## Logistics Operations Analysis Report: Apples, Bananas, and Ice Cream

**Date:** October 26, 2023

**Prepared For:** Logistics Management

**Prepared By:** Logistics Analyst

**Executive Summary:**

This report analyzes the current logistics operations for Apples, Bananas, and Ice Cream, focusing on route efficiency, delivery delays, inventory turnover trends, and key operational bottlenecks. The analysis reveals distinct challenges for each product category, largely driven by their inherent perishability and specific handling requirements. Apples exhibit moderate route efficiency with occasional delays due to seasonality and agricultural factors. Bananas, due to their extreme perishability and ripening process, face significant challenges in maintaining timely deliveries and require highly optimized routes. Ice Cream, a frozen product, demands strict temperature control throughout the supply chain, leading to potential delays and increased costs associated with specialized logistics and inventory management. Key bottlenecks include last-mile delivery for perishable goods, temperature excursions for ice cream, and unpredictable supply for fresh produce.

---

### 1. Apples

**1.1 Route Efficiency:**

*   **Current State:** Route efficiency for apples is generally moderate. Deliveries often originate from regional distribution centers to retail stores. Routes are typically planned based on order volume and delivery windows. However, seasonality plays a significant role, with increased volume during harvest seasons potentially straining existing routes and requiring adjustments.
*   **Inefficiencies Identified:**
    *   **Suboptimal Route Planning:** Static route planning that does not dynamically adjust for real-time traffic or unexpected detours can lead to longer travel times.
    *   **"Milk Run" Inefficiencies:** In some cases, "milk run" delivery models (delivering to multiple stores on one route) can become inefficient if store order volumes vary significantly, leading to excessive time at certain stops or unnecessary mileage.
    *   **Lack of Geofencing/Real-time Tracking Integration:** Limited use of advanced tracking and geofencing can hinder proactive management of delivery times and driver adherence to planned routes.

**1.2 Delivery Delays:**

*   **Current State:** Delivery delays for apples are occasional and often linked to external factors.
*   **Causes of Delays:**
    *   **Agricultural Supply Fluctuations:** Unforeseen weather events, pest outbreaks, or labor shortages at farms can impact the availability of apples, leading to supply chain disruptions and subsequent delivery delays.
    *   **Quality Control Checks:** Rigorous quality checks at various stages of the supply chain (farm, distribution center, retail) can sometimes cause hold-ups.
    *   **Retailer Receiving Schedules:** Delays can occur if retail stores have strict receiving windows that are missed due to unforeseen transit issues.
    *   **Packaging and Palletization:** Inefficient or damaged packaging can slow down loading and unloading processes.

**1.3 Inventory Turnover Trends:**

*   **Current State:** Inventory turnover for apples is generally healthy, driven by consistent consumer demand. However, turnover rates can fluctuate based on varietal popularity and seasonality.
*   **Trends and Observations:**
    *   **Seasonal Peaks:** Higher turnover during peak harvest seasons (late summer/fall) for popular varieties.
    *   **Promotional Impact:** Increased turnover during retail promotions and holiday periods.
    *   **Shelf Life Considerations:** While apples have a relatively good shelf life compared to other fresh produce, prolonged storage can lead to decreased quality and slower turnover, necessitating efficient rotation (First-In, First-Out - FIFO).
    *   **Variety Specificity:** Different apple varieties have varying shelf lives and demand, impacting their individual turnover rates.

**1.4 Key Operational Bottlenecks:**

*   **Agricultural Supply Chain Variability:** The inherent unpredictability of agricultural yields is a primary bottleneck.
*   **Cold Chain Management (Moderate):** While not as critical as for ice cream, maintaining optimal temperature and humidity during transport and storage is still important to prevent spoilage and maintain quality.
*   **Last-Mile Delivery to Retailers:** Ensuring timely and accurate deliveries to a multitude of retail locations, especially during peak hours or in congested urban areas.
*   **Quality Assurance Processes:** Balancing thorough quality checks with the need for rapid movement through the supply chain.

### 2. Bananas

**2.1 Route Efficiency:**

*   **Current State:** Route efficiency for bananas is highly critical and often faces significant challenges due to their extreme perishability and ripening process. Routes must be meticulously planned to minimize transit time from port/ripening facilities to distribution centers and then to retailers.
*   **Inefficiencies Identified:**
    *   **Ripening Window Constraints:** The need to deliver bananas within a very specific ripening window (typically 2-5 days post-ripening) severely limits route flexibility and extends planning complexity.
    *   **Urgency-Driven Routing:** Routes are often dictated by the immediate need to get ripe bananas to market, sometimes bypassing more efficient, but longer, routes.
    *   **Last-Mile Criticality:** The final leg of the journey to retailers is a significant bottleneck. Delays here can result in a complete loss of product.
    *   **Limited Re-routing Capability:** Once a delivery route is set for ripe bananas, significant deviations are often not feasible due to the time-sensitive nature.

**2.2 Delivery Delays:**

*   **Current State:** Delivery delays for bananas are a frequent and significant concern, leading to substantial product spoilage and financial losses.
*   **Causes of Delays:**
    *   **Ripening Process Variability:** Inconsistent ripening times due to ambient temperature fluctuations or inherent fruit variations can throw off delivery schedules.
    *   **Port Congestion and Customs:** Delays at ports of entry for imported bananas are common.
    *   **Transportation Breakdowns:** Any mechanical issue with refrigeration units or vehicles during transit can be catastrophic.
    *   **Retailer Unavailability:** If a retailer cannot accept delivery at the scheduled time, the short shelf life of ripe bananas means they may spoil before they can be rerouted or rescheduled.
    *   **Traffic and Road Conditions:** Standard traffic delays are amplified due to the extreme time sensitivity.

**2.3 Inventory Turnover Trends:**

*   **Current State:** Inventory turnover for bananas is exceptionally high, bordering on just-in-time. The product is effectively "perishable inventory" with a very short usable window.
*   **Trends and Observations:**
    *   **Extremely Short Shelf Life:** Once ripened, bananas have a very limited shelf life (typically 2-3 days at room temperature) before quality degrades significantly.
    *   **"Zero Inventory" Goal:** The ideal scenario is to have bananas move from ripening to retail with minimal holding time.
    *   **High Spoilage Rates:** Delays directly translate to increased spoilage and write-offs, impacting turnover metrics negatively if not managed perfectly.
    *   **Demand Stability:** Consumer demand for bananas is generally stable, which aids in maintaining high turnover, but this stability is threatened by any supply or delivery disruption.

**2.4 Key Operational Bottlenecks:**

*   **Ripening and Ripening Window Management:** This is the single largest bottleneck. Inconsistent ripening or misjudging the window leads to either underripe or overripe product.
*   **Refrigerated Transportation Capacity and Reliability:** Ensuring sufficient, functioning refrigerated trucks and containers is crucial.
*   **Last-Mile Delivery Urgency:** The absolute need for immediate delivery upon ripening.
*   **Port Operations and Customs Clearance:** For imported bananas, these initial stages are critical for timely entry into the ripening and distribution network.
*   **Retailer Readiness:** Ensuring retailers are prepared to receive and display bananas immediately.

### 3. Ice Cream

**3.1 Route Efficiency:**

*   **Current State:** Route efficiency for ice cream is heavily constrained by the absolute necessity of maintaining a frozen state. Routes are planned with minimal stops and optimized for direct, efficient transit to distribution centers and then to retailers with frozen storage.
*   **Inefficiencies Identified:**
    *   **Temperature Control Prioritization:** Route planning must prioritize minimizing time outside of frozen temperatures, often leading to longer, less direct routes if they offer better temperature stability.
    *   **Limited Stop Duration:** Stops at distribution centers and retailers must be as brief as possible to prevent thawing. This can lead to rushed loading/unloading and potential errors.
    *   **Specialized Fleet Requirements:** Reliance on a specialized fleet of refrigerated trucks (reefers) with reliable temperature monitoring systems. Inefficient use or downtime of these assets impacts overall route efficiency.
    *   **Lack of Real-time Temperature Monitoring Integration:** If temperature data is not continuously monitored and transmitted, proactive intervention for potential temperature excursions is delayed.

**3.2 Delivery Delays:**

*   **Current State:** Delivery delays for ice cream are critical and can result in significant product loss and financial impact due to thawing.
*   **Causes of Delays:**
    *   **Refrigeration Unit Failures:** Malfunctions in the truck's refrigeration system are a primary cause of delays and product spoilage.
    *   **Extended Loading/Unloading Times:** Delays in getting the product into or out of frozen storage at any point in the chain.
    *   **Retailer Freezer Issues:** If a retailer's freezer capacity is full or malfunctioning, delivery may be refused, leading to delays and potential product loss.
    *   **Traffic and Road Conditions:** Standard traffic delays are exacerbated by the need to maintain frozen temperatures; even short delays can be problematic if the refrigeration system is struggling.
    *   **"Re-icing" or Maintenance Stops:** Unplanned stops for maintenance or re-icing can disrupt the schedule.

**3.3 Inventory Turnover Trends:**

*   **Current State:** Inventory turnover for ice cream is generally moderate to fast, driven by consumer demand, but it is highly sensitive to seasonality and promotional activity.
*   **Trends and Observations:**
    *   **Seasonal Demand Peaks:** Significantly higher turnover during warmer months (spring/summer).
    *   **Promotional Impact:** Strong spikes in turnover during holidays and special promotions.
    *   **Shelf Life:** While ice cream has a longer theoretical shelf life than fresh produce, its quality degrades over time, especially if subjected to temperature fluctuations. This necessitates efficient stock rotation.
    *   **"Stock-outs" vs. "Spoilage":** The risk is a delicate balance. Holding too much inventory risks spoilage if demand falters or temperature issues arise. Holding too little risks stock-outs during peak demand.
    *   **SKU Proliferation:** A wide variety of flavors and sizes can complicate inventory management and slow down turnover for less popular items.

**3.4 Key Operational Bottlenecks:**

*   **Maintaining the Frozen Cold Chain:** This is the paramount bottleneck. Any break in the -18°C (0°F) or lower temperature chain leads to product quality degradation and potential spoilage.
*   **Refrigerated Fleet Management:** Ensuring the availability, reliability, and proper functioning of specialized refrigerated transport.
*   **Warehouse and Distribution Center Frozen Storage Capacity:** Adequate and reliable frozen storage space at all nodes of the supply chain.
*   **Retailer Frozen Storage and Handling:** Ensuring retailers have sufficient and properly maintained freezer capacity and handling procedures.
*   **Demand Forecasting Accuracy:** Particularly for seasonal fluctuations and promotional spikes, to avoid overstocking or understocking.

---

### Overall Key Operational Bottlenecks Across All Products:

1.  **Last-Mile Delivery Optimization:** For all three product categories, the final delivery to retailers presents challenges. For apples and bananas, it's about timely delivery of perishable goods. For ice cream, it's about maintaining the frozen state during the final leg.
2.  **Cold Chain Integrity:** While the severity varies, maintaining appropriate temperature control is critical for bananas and ice cream, and still important for apples. Any lapse can lead to significant product loss.
3.  **Demand Forecasting Accuracy:** Inaccurate forecasting for seasonal produce (apples), highly perishable items (bananas), and seasonally influenced products (ice cream) leads to inefficiencies, waste, or lost sales.
4.  **Transportation Asset Utilization and Reliability:** Ensuring sufficient availability of appropriate, well-maintained vehicles (especially refrigerated units) and optimizing their usage across different product needs.
5.  **Inventory Management and Rotation:** Effectively managing inventory to minimize spoilage, obsolescence, and stock-outs, especially for items with short or variable shelf lives.
6.  **Information Flow and Real-time Visibility:** Lack of real-time data on inventory levels, vehicle locations, and temperature monitoring hinders proactive decision-making and problem-solving.

---

### Recommendations for Improvement (General):

*   **Implement Advanced Route Optimization Software:** Utilize dynamic routing that considers real-time traffic, weather, and delivery priorities.
*   **Enhance Cold Chain Monitoring:** Deploy IoT sensors for continuous temperature tracking with real-time alerts for all refrigerated shipments.
*   **Improve Demand Forecasting Models:** Integrate historical sales data, market trends, weather forecasts, and promotional calendars.
*   **Invest in Fleet Modernization and Maintenance:** Ensure a reliable fleet, particularly for refrigerated vehicles, with robust preventative maintenance programs.
*   **Optimize Warehouse and DC Operations:** Streamline loading/unloading processes and ensure adequate, well-maintained frozen/temperature-controlled storage.
*   **Strengthen Collaboration with Retailers:** Improve communication regarding delivery windows, receiving capacity, and potential issues.
*   **Explore Technology for Perishables:** Investigate technologies like controlled atmosphere packaging or advanced ripening techniques for bananas.

This report provides a foundational analysis. Further in-depth studies on specific routes, carrier performance, and warehouse operations would yield more granular insights and targeted improvement strategies.

# Optimization Strategy Plan

## Comprehensive Logistics Optimization Strategy for Apples, Bananas, and Ice Cream

This strategy outlines actionable steps to optimize logistics operations for Apples, Bananas, and Ice Cream, addressing identified inefficiencies and leveraging best practices in route optimization, demand forecasting, and inventory management.

---

### 1. Apples Optimization Strategy

**1.1 Route Optimization Ideas:**

*   **Dynamic Route Planning with Real-time Data:**
    *   **Action:** Implement route optimization software that integrates real-time traffic data, weather forecasts, and driver feedback.
    *   **Implementation:** Utilize a system that can dynamically re-route vehicles if significant delays are detected or if a more efficient route becomes available due to changing conditions.
    *   **Specifics:** For "milk runs," the software should analyze order variability and suggest optimal sequencing or even dedicated routes for high-volume stores if it proves more efficient. Integrate geofencing to ensure drivers adhere to planned routes and to trigger alerts for deviations.
*   **Load Consolidation and Backhauling:**
    *   **Action:** Analyze historical delivery data to identify opportunities for consolidating smaller orders into larger, more efficient loads. Explore backhauling opportunities from retail locations or suppliers.
    *   **Implementation:** Use route optimization software to identify potential backhaul loads that align with existing routes and delivery schedules, thereby reducing empty miles.
*   **Tiered Delivery Windows:**
    *   **Action:** Work with retailers to establish tiered delivery windows, offering slightly better pricing or guaranteed delivery slots for less time-sensitive deliveries, allowing for more flexible routing.
    *   **Implementation:** Communicate the benefits of flexible delivery to retailers and adjust routing based on these agreed-upon windows.

**1.2 Inventory Management Improvements:**

*   **Data-Driven Inventory Rotation (FIFO with Shelf-Life Consideration):**
    *   **Action:** Enhance existing FIFO (First-In, First-Out) systems to incorporate varietal shelf-life data.
    *   **Implementation:** Implement warehouse management systems (WMS) that track inventory by lot/batch and expiry date, prioritizing older stock or stock with shorter remaining shelf life for outgoing shipments. Use scanning technology at receiving and shipping to ensure accurate rotation.
*   **Dynamic Safety Stock Adjustments:**
    *   **Action:** Adjust safety stock levels for apples based on real-time supply forecasts, seasonality, and historical demand variability.
    *   **Implementation:** Utilize demand forecasting tools that can predict short-term supply fluctuations (e.g., due to weather affecting harvests) and adjust safety stock accordingly to prevent stock-outs without overstocking.
*   **Variety-Specific Inventory Tracking:**
    *   **Action:** Maintain detailed inventory records for each apple variety, recognizing their differing shelf lives and demand patterns.
    *   **Implementation:** Ensure the WMS can differentiate and manage inventory at the SKU level for each apple variety.

**1.3 Technology or Automation Recommendations:**

*   **IoT Sensors for Environmental Monitoring:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Deploy IoT sensors in transport vehicles and storage facilities to monitor temperature and humidity.
    *   **Benefit:** Proactive identification of conditions that could lead to spoilage, allowing for immediate corrective action.
*   **Advanced Route Optimization Software:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Invest in a robust route optimization platform with dynamic re-routing capabilities, real-time traffic integration, and geofencing.
    *   **Benefit:** Significant reduction in travel time, fuel costs, and improved on-time delivery rates.
*   **Warehouse Management System (WMS) Upgrade:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Ensure the WMS supports advanced inventory tracking, FIFO with shelf-life considerations, and integration with route optimization software.
    *   **Benefit:** Improved inventory accuracy, reduced waste, and streamlined warehouse operations.

**1.4 Expected Benefits:**

*   **Cost Reduction:** 5-10% reduction in fuel costs and transportation expenses due to optimized routes and reduced mileage. 3-5% reduction in spoilage losses.
*   **Time Savings:** 10-15% reduction in average delivery times. Improved driver productivity.
*   **Efficiency Gains:** Enhanced on-time delivery performance (aiming for 95%+). Better utilization of fleet assets. Reduced administrative overhead for route planning.

### 2. Bananas Optimization Strategy

**2.1 Route Optimization Ideas:**

*   **Ripening-Synchronized Routing:**
    *   **Action:** Develop a tightly integrated system where ripening schedules directly inform delivery routes and departure times.
    *   **Implementation:** Utilize ripening forecasting software that communicates expected readiness dates to the logistics planning system. Routes should be generated to pick up from ripening facilities precisely when the product is ready and to deliver within the narrow optimal window.
*   **Expedited Last-Mile Delivery Network:**
    *   **Action:** Establish dedicated, expedited routes for ripe bananas, potentially using smaller, more agile vehicles for the last mile in congested urban areas.
    *   **Implementation:** Prioritize banana deliveries in the routing algorithm, treating them as high-priority, time-critical shipments. Consider partnerships with specialized last-mile delivery providers if internal capacity is insufficient.
*   **Contingency Routing for Retailer Unavailability:**
    *   **Action:** Pre-define alternative delivery points or protocols for scenarios where a retailer cannot accept a delivery of ripe bananas.
    *   **Implementation:** The routing system should have pre-approved secondary destinations (e.g., other nearby stores of the same chain, or a dedicated holding facility with rapid redistribution capabilities) to minimize loss.

**2.2 Inventory Management Improvements:**

*   **"Flow-Through" Ripening and Distribution:**
    *   **Action:** Strive for a "zero inventory" model for ripe bananas, aiming for immediate transfer from ripening to distribution to retail.
    *   **Implementation:** Optimize ripening room capacity and loading dock scheduling to ensure seamless transition. Minimize holding time at any stage post-ripening.
*   **Real-time Ripeness Monitoring and Demand Alignment:**
    *   **Action:** Implement systems that provide real-time visibility into banana ripeness levels and align immediate delivery schedules with actual demand.
    *   **Implementation:** Use sensors or visual inspection data integrated with the WMS/ERP to track ripeness progression. Adjust delivery plans dynamically based on real-time ripeness and actual retailer orders.
*   **High-Turnover Buffer Stock Management:**
    *   **Action:** Maintain a minimal, highly managed buffer stock of *unripe* bananas at strategic locations to mitigate port delays or initial supply disruptions.
    *   **Implementation:** This buffer should be managed strictly to ensure it moves through the ripening process before reaching its own expiry.

**2.3 Technology or Automation Recommendations:**

*   **Ripening Management Software:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Invest in specialized software that accurately predicts ripening times based on fruit condition, ambient temperature, and ethylene levels.
    *   **Benefit:** Significantly improves the accuracy of delivery scheduling and reduces product loss due to over- or under-ripening.
*   **Integrated Cold Chain Monitoring (Advanced):**
    *   **Recommendation:** Deploy advanced IoT sensors in all transport and storage for bananas, with real-time alerts for any temperature deviation that could impact ripening or spoilage.
    *   **Benefit:** Critical for preventing catastrophic spoilage and ensuring product quality.
*   **Predictive Analytics for Port/Customs Delays:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Utilize predictive analytics tools that can forecast potential delays at ports and customs based on historical data, vessel schedules, and current congestion levels.
    *   **Benefit:** Allows for proactive adjustments to ripening schedules and inventory planning, mitigating the impact of external delays.
*   **Automated Loading/Unloading Systems (where feasible):**
    *   **Recommendation:** Explore automation for loading and unloading at ripening facilities and distribution centers to speed up transit and reduce manual handling.
    *   **Benefit:** Reduces dwell time and the risk of damage or temperature breaches.

**2.4 Expected Benefits:**

*   **Cost Reduction:** 15-25% reduction in spoilage and write-offs. Significant reduction in waste-related costs.
*   **Time Savings:** 20-30% improvement in on-time delivery of ripe bananas. Reduced lead times from port to retail.
*   **Efficiency Gains:** Near "just-in-time" delivery of a highly perishable product. Improved customer satisfaction due to consistent availability of optimally ripe bananas. Enhanced ability to manage import supply chain risks.

### 3. Ice Cream Optimization Strategy

**3.1 Route Optimization Ideas:**

*   **Temperature-Centric Dynamic Routing:**
    *   **Action:** Prioritize routes that minimize time outside of optimal frozen temperatures, even if they are slightly longer in distance.
    *   **Implementation:** Route optimization software must be configured to factor in the "frozen time" constraint. This includes minimizing stops, direct routes, and considering ambient temperatures along potential routes.
*   **Optimized Stop Duration and Loading/Unloading:**
    *   **Action:** Implement strict protocols and potentially technology to reduce loading and unloading times at distribution centers and retailers.
    *   **Implementation:** Utilize pre-cooled loading docks, ensure efficient pallet handling, and train staff on rapid loading/unloading procedures. The routing system should flag routes with excessive historical stop durations for review.
*   **Fleet Balancing and Scheduling:**
    *   **Action:** Optimize the deployment of the specialized refrigerated fleet to ensure maximum utilization and availability for high-demand periods.
    *   **Implementation:** Use fleet management software to track vehicle locations, temperatures, and maintenance schedules, integrating this data into route planning to avoid assigning a vehicle with upcoming maintenance to a critical frozen delivery.

**3.2 Inventory Management Improvements:**

*   **Demand-Driven Frozen Inventory Management:**
    *   **Action:** Implement sophisticated demand forecasting that accounts for seasonality, promotions, and local events to optimize frozen inventory levels.
    *   **Implementation:** Use forecasting models that integrate historical sales, weather forecasts (especially for summer peaks), and promotional calendars. Set optimal stock levels at DCs and retail to balance stock-out risk with freezer capacity and spoilage risk.
*   **First-Expired, First-Out (FEFO) for Quality:**
    *   **Action:** Shift from strict FIFO to FEFO (First-Expired, First-Out) for ice cream, as quality degrades over time even if frozen.
    *   **Implementation:** WMS must track expiry dates, not just receipt dates, to ensure older stock (by expiry) is shipped first. This is crucial given potential quality degradation from minor temperature fluctuations.
*   **SKU Rationalization and Slotting Optimization:**
    *   **Action:** Analyze sales data to identify slow-moving SKUs and consider rationalization. Optimize freezer slotting to place high-turnover items in easily accessible locations.
    *   **Implementation:** Regularly review SKU performance and freezer layout to improve picking efficiency and reduce dwell time within the freezer.

**3.3 Technology or Automation Recommendations:**

*   **Real-time Cold Chain Monitoring with AI Alerts:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Deploy advanced IoT sensors with AI capabilities that not only track temperature but also predict potential failures or excursions based on patterns.
    *   **Benefit:** Proactive prevention of thawing and spoilage, saving significant product value and cost. Alerts can trigger route adjustments or immediate diversion.
*   **Fleet Management System (FMS) with Reefer Telematics:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Invest in an FMS that integrates seamlessly with refrigerated truck telematics, providing real-time data on temperature, fuel levels for refrigeration units, and diagnostic information.
    *   **Benefit:** Enhanced fleet utilization, proactive maintenance scheduling, and immediate alerts for refrigeration system issues.
*   **Warehouse Management System (WMS) with FEFO and Slotting Optimization:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Upgrade WMS to support FEFO inventory tracking and dynamic slotting based on product velocity.
    *   **Benefit:** Reduced spoilage due to quality degradation and improved picking efficiency in frozen storage.
*   **Automated Guided Vehicles (AGVs) or Robots in Frozen Warehouses:**
    *   **Recommendation:** Explore automation for moving pallets within frozen warehouses to reduce human exposure to extreme cold and speed up operations.
    *   **Benefit:** Increased operational speed, reduced labor costs in extreme conditions, and improved safety.

**3.4 Expected Benefits:**

*   **Cost Reduction:** 8-12% reduction in product loss due to thawing or quality degradation. 5-10% reduction in fleet maintenance and operational costs.
*   **Time Savings:** 10-15% improvement in on-time delivery for frozen goods. Reduced time spent on manual inventory checks and route planning.
*   **Efficiency Gains:** Enhanced cold chain integrity. Improved freezer utilization and storage capacity. Better alignment of inventory with seasonal demand peaks, leading to higher sales during those periods.
